# Model specification

Plain-language statement of what the app computes, so every number on screen can be traced.

## 1. The envelope

Two ends, both settable.

**Near end (when could chemistry start).** Three ways to set it:

1. Pick a named planetary event: end of the magma ocean (4.40 Ga), the Moon-forming impact
   (4.36 Ga), first evidence of liquid water (4.40 Ga, one detrital zircon), earliest continuous
   habitability (4.34 Ga).
2. Set a maximum tolerable surface temperature. The app reads a cooling curve back to the
   oldest age at which the surface was at or below that temperature. The curve is Tier C and
   its anchor points are editable. It is illustrative, not a result.
3. Type an age directly.

**Far end (when LUCA had to exist).** Pick the evidence you accept:

- Moody et al. 2024 molecular clock, central 4.2 Ga, reported range 4.33 to 4.09 Ga.
- Conservative clock estimate, 3.8 Ga.
- Isua graphite and inclusions, 3.8 to 3.7 Ga, contested.
- Jack Hills zircon graphite, 4.1 Ga, contested.
- Type an age directly.

**Budget** = near end minus far end, in Myr.

## 2. Temperature enters twice

Once through the envelope, and once through the chemistry. Every handoff time is corrected
with the Arrhenius relation

    tau(T) = tau(T_ref) * exp( (Ea / R) * (1/T - 1/T_ref) )

with T in kelvin and T_ref = 298.15 K. Two separate activation energies:

- **Transfer**, default 50 kJ per mole, Tier C. Generic value for a catalysed surface reaction.
- **Loss**, default 63 kJ per mole, Tier B. Derived in the app from measured RNA
  phosphodiester hydrolysis: half-life 4 years at 25 C and 9 days at 100 C gives
  Ea = R * ln(162) / (1/298.15 - 1/373.15) = 62.8 kJ per mole.

Because the two activation energies differ, cooling does not shift transfer and loss by the
same factor. That is the point: the Damkohler number itself is temperature dependent, so a
cold start does not simply slow everything down uniformly, it changes which rungs pass.

## 3. Chemistry to probability to duration

For each rung the model takes a representative handoff from the Fig2 table, which supplies
tau_transfer and tau_loss as ranges with a tier and a source.

    Da   = tau_loss / tau_transfer            Damkohler number, dimensionless
    p    = Da / (1 + Da)                      probability that the species is handed on
                                              rather than lost

This is not an extra assumption bolted on. It is what the handoff criterion already says. If
transfer is much faster than loss, Da is large and p approaches 1. If loss is much faster,
p approaches Da, which is small. On the diagonal, Da = 1 and p = 0.5.

Joint probability over the rungs that have to succeed in the same place and the right order:

    p_joint = product of p over the selected rungs

Attempts are generated by the driving cycle in each independent vessel:

    attempt rate = N_sites / T_cycle

Expected waiting time for the required number of independent successes:

    T3 = n_successes_needed * T_cycle / (p_joint * N_sites)

## 4. The four terms of a required duration

Exactly as in the manuscript and the Method_time_budgets sheet.

| Term | What it is | How the app gets it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Setting availability and recurrence | Scenario parameter, Tier B. Multiplied by a land-uncertainty factor when the scenario needs subaerial land. |
| T2 | Chemistry, Class 1 and Class 2 steps | Computed: sum of temperature-corrected tau_transfer over the selected rungs, converted to Myr. |
| T3 | Coincidence waiting time | Computed from the formula above. |
| T4 | Slow biological steps, Class 3 | Computed from generation time, population size and the number of generations to fixation. Default lands on 1 to 10 Myr. |

    required duration = T1 + T2 + T3 + T4

Verdict against the envelope: fits, fits at the fast end, or needs the longer budget.

## 5. The preservation rung, S6

S6 sits after S5 and does **not** consume the pre-LUCA budget. It is a filter on the evidence,
not on life. Two gates.

**S6a, ecosystem persistence.** LUCA was not alone. A community survives only if it
regenerates its limiting nutrient faster than the vessel loses it:

    Da_eco = tau_vessel_lifetime / tau_nutrient_recycling

and the number of turnovers is that same ratio. One turnover is a population. Thousands of
turnovers is an ecosystem with cross-feeding.

**S6b, rock-record preservation.** A molecule class becomes a biomarker only if

    tau_degradation(T_burial) > age of the rock

with tau_degradation Arrhenius-corrected from a measured half-life, and a hard ceiling from
thermal maturity: above about 150 C the oil window ends, and above greenschist metamorphism
carbon exchanges and even the isotopic signal degrades.

## 6. Export

- SVG for every figure, which is the primary format because it is vector and editable.
- PNG for the same figures, rendered from the SVG, with a fallback to the SVG download if the
  browser blocks canvas readback.
- CSV of the current run, one row per scenario with all four terms.
- JSON of the full parameter state, so a run is reproducible.
- JSON import, so elements, handoffs and references can be added as new papers appear.

## Version 6: guided entry, T5 ecosystem, time left and restarts

App structure. A switch at the top chooses Guided (default) or Full workbench. Guided has four pages:
1. Start here: three choices (window, chemistry preset, community size) and six setting cards with the window drawn to scale.
2. Follow one setting: a nine-step story (setting, S1 to S4, waiting for luck, copying with selection, ecosystem, LUCA and time left) with a scene drawing per setting, a form-against-survive bar and a difficulty pill per step.
3. Molecules and cycles: the pass or perish picker for the twelve handoffs, and a setting by cycle map of total time.
4. LUCA's ecosystem and time left: X kinds, Y links, the T5 rules, and the time-left figure.
Each guided page has a folded "More detailed settings" panel with the same sliders as the workbench. The workbench keeps all ten pages plus page 11 (the ecosystem page). Open the workbench directly with ?mode=workbench.

T5, building LUCA's ecosystem (Tier C), same for every setting:
- splitting: t_div = ln(X) / lambda, lambda per lineage per Myr (default 0.1; modern bacteria 0.03 to 0.05, Louca et al. 2018)
- linking: pairs = X(X-1)/2, C = Y / pairs, t_int = -ln(1-C) / r, r per pair per Myr (default 1)
- T5 = max(t_div, t_int) if links form while kinds appear, else t_div + t_int; plus an optional extra time; or set directly; or left out
- total = T1 + T2 + T3 + T4 + T5

Time left and restarts:
- time left = window - total
- one try = T2 + T3 + T4 + T5 (or T1 + ... + T5 if a restart must wait for the setting again)
- tries that fit = floor(time open for tries / one try); tries that stopped = tries - 1
- chance it fits = P(Gamma(n, mean T3) <= window - T1 - T2 - T4 - T5); exact for n up to 20,000, normal approximation above
- not counted: tries running in parallel at different places

## Version 6.1: hard steps, cycles explained, confidence stars

It opens with a side-by-side table of the default and hard molecules for S2 to S4 (live chances at the working temperature, free water) and the billion-successes rule, then an element grid for S0 to S5 (easy, medium, hard from data/model.json elements), with tags where the default (teal) and hard (red) chemistry take their molecule.

## Version 6.2: the cycle as currency, and what matters most

**The cycle as currency.** A switch (guided page 3, and the workbench parameters page) gives the driving cycle a second role. As a clock it only sets how often a place gets a try (T3). As a currency a drying cycle also pays for the joining step, the RNA oligomer handoff (H10 free, H11 on a mineral; `cycle_currency` in data/model.json), wherever it is used (S5 by default).

    time to form one bond      tau_f = T_cycle / y
    time the product survives  1 / tau_s = 1 / tau_chem + d / T_cycle
    Da = tau_s / tau_f,  chance per bond = Da / (1 + Da),  chance per product = (chance per bond) ^ bonds
    the step adds bonds * tau_f to T2

- y, yield per cycle (Tier B). Default from Eiby 2025: five one-hour cycles at 80 C, pH 2 to 3, no catalyst, 0.1 to 1.2 per cent by mass, at most four units; per cycle 0.02 to 0.24 per cent, default 0.069 per cent. Alternatives: Rajamani et al. 2008 with lipids (about 0.9 per cent per cycle at most) and Caimi et al. 2025 with activated 2',3'-cyclic nucleotides (about 2 to 65 per cent per cycle, default 6.5), both as summarised in Eiby 2025, Table 2.3.
- d, loss per cycle (Tier C). Default 0.1 per cent; not yet measured.
- bonds needed per product (Tier C). Default 1 (a dimer); 3 is a tetramer; 28 is a 29-unit polymerase ribozyme.
- Only cycles that dry pay (`dries` in data/model.json: wet and dry, day and night, tides, raft beaching), unless "every cycle" is chosen as a stress test.
- In a long series the yield levels off at y / (y + d + T_cycle / tau_chem), which equals the chance per bond. So a yield-against-cycle-number series sets both numbers: the first cycles give y, the level reached gives the chance per bond.
- Default is "clock only", so all earlier results are unchanged unless the switch is used.

**What matters most** (guided page 5, workbench page 12). For the current choices, every input that can change the total is moved on its own from the low to the high end of its plausible range (SPEC lo and hi), with all other inputs as set. Two categorical inputs are added: every driving cycle that fits the setting, and mineral surfaces on or off. For each input the page shows how many times the total changes (largest over the settings in scope), which settings move into or out of the window, its confidence stars and the data that would set it. The best target for new data is the highest-ranked input with one star. Not tested: the window, the chemistry choice and the successes needed. Inputs with less than 5 per cent effect are listed separately. CSV download.

**Version 6.2.1.** The currency table on guided page 3 now shows, for each setting, the wait for luck (T3) and the chemistry time (T2) with the cycle as a clock only and then as clock and currency, in readable units. The total column also shows the part that the cycle cannot touch (T1 + T4 + T5), and a note below the table explains why the total often stays the same: T4 + T5 alone is at least about 17 Myr. The column "Time to make one product" was removed, since the T2 column now carries it. Times above 10,000 Gyr are written in powers of ten.

## Version 7: first update for the Geobiology group at GLOBE

A new page, "Start and supply" (guided page 6, workbench page 13), adds four things. Each is off, or set as before, by default, so every earlier result is unchanged until a switch is used (checked: the totals for all six settings match version 6.2.1 exactly, for the default, hard-with-surfaces and hard-without-surfaces chemistry).

**1. The start built from accretion and volatiles** (`nearMode = "build"`, also on the workbench envelope page as "From accretion").

    start = Moon-forming impact age - (surface solidifies + max(CO2 ingassing, volatile re-supply)) / 1000

- Moon-forming impact, `moonAgeGa`: 4.51 Ga (Barboni et al. 2017), 4.47 Ga default (70 to 120 Myr after the first solids, Nature Reviews 2022), 4.35 Ga (lunar crust ages, now read as tidal remelting by Nimmo et al. 2024). Tier B.
- Surface solid after, `solidifyMyr`: 2 Myr default (Zahnle et al. 2007; Elkins-Tanton 2011), up to 150 Myr for the whole mantle, 30 to 500 Myr with tidal heating (arXiv 2511.00952, preprint). Tier B.
- CO2 back into the rock, `co2DrawdownMyr`: 10 to 100 Myr (Zahnle et al. 2007, 2010; Sleep 2010), default 30; up to 160 Myr for a wet, uneven mantle (Miyazaki and Korenaga 2022); 1 Myr for a dense atmosphere with water at once (Loth et al. 2026, preprint). Tier B.
- How Earth grew, `accretionPath`: pebble accretion (Johansen et al. 2021, 2023; Onyett et al. 2023), slow growth with volatiles kept, or slow growth with volatiles lost in giant impacts. Only the last adds a re-supply wait, from late accretion falling off as exp(-(t / 47 Myr)^0.8) (the chronology used by Abramov et al. 2026): wait = 47 (-ln(1 - f))^(1/0.8) Myr for a share f (`lateShare`, Tier C, default 0.5, which gives about 30 Myr; 0.9 gives about 133 Myr).
- Dated evidence is shown as checks, not inputs: liquid water 4.40 Ga (Wilde et al. 2001), widespread water 4.3 Ga (Mojzsis et al. 2001), no global sterilisation after 4.4 Ga and a sweet spot centred near 4.33 Ga (Abramov et al. 2026), fresh water and land 4.0 Ga (Gamaleldien et al. 2024). A Moon younger than 4.40 Ga is flagged.
- Results with LUCA at 4.2 Ga and the default chemistry: the built start ranges from 4.507 Ga (early Moon, water at once, window 307 Myr) through 4.438 Ga (defaults, 238 Myr) and 4.308 Ga (wet uneven mantle, 108 Myr) to 4.248 Ga (late Moon plus 100 Myr of ingassing, 48 Myr; only the vent, impact and raft settings fit). Whole-mantle solidification plus 160 Myr of ingassing closes the window.

**2. Ocean depth and dry land** (`oceanView`). A preset for the land factor on T1 of the wet and dry pools: shallow ocean with land at once (Loth et al. 2026) sets 1; a deep water world (Korenaga 2021) sets 20; "as set" keeps the value on the parameters page.

**3. Boron and the ribose step** (`riboseStep`, `borateFactor`, `borateWhere`). When on, H6 (formaldehyde to sugars) is added after S2 as "ribose kept": its chance multiplies the joint chance and its formation time adds to T2. Borate multiplies its survival time by 2.1 (40 mM) or 16 (80 mM) (Furukawa et al. 2013, 45 C, pH 12), with 48 as an upper bound (branched pentoses, Benner et al. 2010), only in settings where borate can collect (`borate_concentrates` in data/model.json: pools, impact, raft, icy; not the vent or panspermia; Grew et al. 2011). At 70 C the ribose step alone passes 33 per cent of the time; with borate at 16 times, 89 per cent. Totals move only where T3 is large: with the hard chemistry and no surfaces, the step makes the vent total 2.3 times longer (49 to 113 Myr), and the pools and icy totals about 3 times longer, and borate wins most of that back where it collects. Boron is also added as a tenth row of the element matrix.

**4. Delivery from space as a shortcut** (`deliverySource`, `deliveryCovers`, `deliveryRatePerMyr`, `deliveryWindowYr`, `deliveryWhere`, `deliveryBlocked`). A second route that runs next to making things in place:

    chance per delivery q = 1 - (1 - p_rest)^m,  m = window / T_cycle
    successes per second = (p_joint * N_sites / T_cycle) + (deliveries per second * q)
    T3 = successes needed / successes per second

- Sources: meteorites into ponds (covers S2 and the ribose step; Pearce et al. 2017; Glavin et al. 2025; Furukawa et al. 2025), interstellar organics and peptides (covers S4 as organic carbon; Ioppolo et al. 2021; Krasnokutski et al. 2022; InterCat authors 2026), comet HCN (covers no rate-limited step; Todd and Oberg 2020). The covered steps can be changed.
- Rate 0.02 to 11 deliveries per Myr into warm little ponds, default 0.5; usable for 0.5 to 7 years (Pearce et al. 2017). Receiving settings: pools, impact crater lakes, icy surfaces (`receives_delivery`).
- Result: deliveries are rare compared with the tries a setting gets in place, so delivery wins only when the covered steps, made in place, have a chance below a break-even value (about 1 in 5 x 10^10 for the pools at the defaults), or cannot happen in place at all ("only by delivery"). In that case the pools wait about 2 Myr for one useful delivery (total 84 to 86 Myr), and a billion successes would need about 2 x 10^9 Myr.

**What matters most** now also tests the start inputs when the start is built. Because they move the window, not the total, their factor compares total against window. The ribose and delivery inputs are tested when switched on.

### Version 8.1: scenario 7 describes one physical place
By default scenario 7 now chooses one of seven places: the five real settings (panspermia is a delivery route, so it is left out) and two hybrids, an alkaline spring feeding a pond on land (SC1 plus SC2) and a shallow alkaline vent on a tidal shore (SC1 plus SC4). Each place has ranges for temperature, pH and salinity, plus its own minerals, cycles and build-up places. Each handoff has stated needs, for example H3 needs 50 C or less and pH 7.5 to 10, H9 needs an Fe or Ni sulfide surface, and H12 needs volcanic glass. A hybrid takes T1 from its slower parent and its sites from its rarer parent. The earlier free search is still available as "free mix", and its conflicts are listed.
